- The primary objective of any for-profit management is to maximize owner's wealth, which translates to maximizing the price of shares.
- How a business is structured determines who is liable, how it is taxed, and how it raises capital.

# Sole Proprietorship

**Sole Proprietorship**
- A business owned and operated by one person.
- No legal separation between the owner and the business.
- Owner receives all profits directly.

**Unlimited liability**
- The owner is personally responsible for all debts and obligations.
- Creditors can go after personal assets (house, car, savings).

- Taxed via ==pass-through taxation== — business income is reported on the owner's personal tax return.
	- No corporate tax. Profits taxed once at the individual rate.

- Easiest and cheapest entity to form, no formal registration beyond local licenses.
- Cannot sell equity. Funding is limited to personal capital and loans.
- Entity dies with the owner, no continuity of life.

# Partnership

**Partnership**
- A business owned by two or more individuals.

**General Partnership (GP)**
- All partners share management responsibilities.
- All partners have unlimited liability.

**Limited Partnership (LP)**
- At least one ==general partner== with unlimited liability who manages the business.
- ==Limited partners== contribute capital but do not manage — their liability is capped at their investment.

- Pass-through taxation — profits and losses flow to each partner's personal return based on their share.
- Partners are bound by a partnership agreement that defines profit splits, roles, and exit terms.

# Corporation

**Corporation**
- A legal entity separate from its owners.
- The business itself can own property, sue, and be sued.
- Owners hold shares of stock. Ownership is transferable and can be sold in [[financial assets]].
- ==Limited liability==, Shareholders can only lose what they invested. Personal assets are protected.
- Managed by a board of directors elected by shareholders, who then appoint officers to run daily operations.
- Continuity of life, the entity survives regardless of ownership changes.

* ***C Corporation (C-Corp)**
	- Subject to ==double taxation== — the corporation pays corporate tax on profits, then shareholders pay personal tax on dividends.

**S Corporation (S-Corp)**
- Pass-through taxation like a partnership, no corporate-level tax.
	- Restricted to 100 or fewer shareholders, all must be U.S. citizens/residents.
	- Only one class of stock allowed.
- Corporations can raise capital by issuing stock or bonds on [[financial assets]].

# Limited Liability Company (LLC)

**LLC**
- A hybrid entity that combines the limited liability of a corporation with the pass-through taxation of a partnership.
- Members (owners) are not personally liable for business debts.
- Profits pass through to members' personal tax returns — no double taxation by default.

- Can elect to be taxed as a C-Corp or S-Corp if beneficial.
- Fewer formalities than a corporation, no required board of directors or annual meetings.
- Ownership is held through ==membership interests==, not shares.
