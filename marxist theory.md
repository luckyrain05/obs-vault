- Marx's work spans decades and multiple texts. The foundation is the *Communist Manifesto* (1848, with Engels) and *Das Kapital* (1867–1894), summarize below.

# Material Conditions

**Material Condition**
- How a society produces and distributes goods
- Marx argues that material conditions determine its social structures, politics, laws, and ideas. Not the other way around.

- A society's ==base== is its economic system.
- The ==superstructure== is everything built on top of the base.
	- Government, law, religion, culture, philosophy.
	- The superstructure reflects and reinforces the interests of whoever controls the base.

# Historial Materialism

- History moves through stages, each defined by its mode of production.
	- Feudalism → Capitalism → Socialism → Communism.
	- Each stage contains internal contradictions that eventually destroy it and give rise to the next. 

# Class

- The requirements for producing a product is called ==means of production==
- Class is defined by one's relationship to the means of production.
	- If you own means of production, you are ==bourgeoisie==.
	- If you sell your labor to survive, you are ==proletariat==.

- Marx acknowledges intermediate layers. For example, most workers today own [[stocks]], which technically makes them owners of companies. 
- But Marx argues capitalism polarizes society into these two camps over time.

# Division of Labor

- ==Division of labor== is the splitting of production into specialized tasks.
**Marx distinguishes two forms:**
- **Social division of labor**
	- Different industries and occupations existing in society (farmers, builders, teachers). This exists in every society.
- **Technical division of labor**
	- Breaking a single production process into fragmented, repetitive tasks within a workplace. This is specific to capitalism.

- Under communism, Marx envision an abolishment of the technical division of labor.

# Alienation

- ==Alienation== is Marx's term for the separation workers experience under capitalism.
- Alienation is not a feeling — Marx treats it as a structural condition built into the wage-labor relationship.

**Alienation from the product**
- The worker makes something that belongs to the capitalist. The product confronts the worker as something foreign.

**Alienation from the process**
- The worker has no control over how they work. The labor process is dictated by the employer.
- Marx emphasizes this alienation in his critic of the technical division of labor. 
- A worker is alienated from the process of creation when they are designated to singular, repetitive tasks.

 **Alienation from species-being** 
 - Humans are naturally creative, productive beings. Capitalism reduces labor to a survival mechanism, stripping it of meaning.

**Alienation from other workers**
- competition for wages pits workers against each other instead of fostering cooperation.

# Exploitation and Surplus Value

**Labor Power**
- The worker's capacity to work, which they sell to the capitalist for a wage.
**Surplus Value**
- The difference between the value a worker produces and the wage they receive.
- The bourgeoisie reaps surplus value as profit.
- This is not theft in the legal sense, the worker agreed to the wage. Marx's point is that the system is structured so the worker has no real choice. They must sell labor-power or starve.

# The Falling Rate of Profit

- Marx argues capitalists increasingly replace human labor with machinery to stay competitive.

**Organic composition of capital**

- The ratio of investment in machinery/materials (constant capital) to investment in labor (variable capital).
- Marx argues only labor produces surplus value. As capitalists invest more in machines and less in labor, the rate of profit tends to fall.
	- The drive for efficiency then undermines the source of profit itself, thus as capitalist continues to expand through automation, profit falls.
	- Capitalists counteract this temporarily.
		- Intensifying exploitation.
		- Expanding into new markets.
		- Cheapening the elements of constant capital through innovation.

# Crises of Overproduction

- Capitalism periodically produces more than the market can absorb.
- Workers' wages are kept low to maximize surplus value,  but this means workers cannot afford to buy back what they produce.
- Marx argues that a downward spiral is formed. Goods pile up thus driving down demand, factories shut down due to lack of revenue and fires workers, which further drives down demand to do their low wage.

# Concentration of Capital

- Marx sees crises of overproduction as integral to capitalism.
- Each crisis concentrates capital into fewer hands (weaker firms fail, stronger ones absorb them) and expands the proletariat (displaced owners become workers).

# Class Consciousness

**Class in itself**

- A class that exists objectively (workers share the same relationship to production) but whose members do not yet recognize their shared interests.

**Class for itself**

- A class that has become aware of its collective position and organizes to act on it.
- Class consciousness occurs through shared experiences.
	- Factories concentrate workers together.
	- Repeated crises demonstrate that their suffering is systemic, not individual.
	- Communication and organization spread awareness.

# Cultural Revolution

- Marx argues that revolution is not a choice, rather an inevitable event.
- The proletariat, as the majority class with nothing to lose, overthrows the bourgeoisie and seizes the means of production.

# Socialism

- Socialism, as a term, is coined by later writers and theorists. Marx referred to it as  lower phase of communist society.
- It is a transitional stage between communism and capitalism.

- Core criteria for Marx's definition of socialism.

**Dictatorship of the proletariat**

- Expropriate the means of production from the bourgeoisie. Reorganize production under collective/social ownership.
- State and class still exists. But power is held by the proletariat.
- Equal access to labor, but compensation depends on productivity and need.
- Worker ownership does not solve inter-firm dependency. If firm A's production depends on firm B, firm B holds the means of production to firm A.
- Marx's vision is more radical than firm ownership, entire sectors are dismantled, [[business entities]] ceases to exist.

**Labor Vouchers**

- Replaces currency. Workers use it to exchange for resources. Destroyed upon exchange and cannot be recirculated.

# Communism

- Communism is what emerges after the socialist transition has done its work. Marx define it as a classless, stateless society.

**Classless**

- A group cannot own the means of production privately. Production is collectively owned and managed. With no ownership divide, there is no basis for class.

**Stateless**

- Marx defines the state as a tool of class oppression. When classes disappear, the state has no function and withers away.
- This does not equal to the abolishment of governance. Rather, state functions that oppress the proletariat is removed.
- Marx distinguishes governance from administration. He defines governance as state, and administrations coordinate society.

**Moneyless**

- Production is for use, not exchange. Goods are distributed based on need, not purchased. Thus, currency is abolished.
- Communism heavily depends on automation and material abundance. 
- Technical division of labor is abolished by outsourcing repetitive tasks to automation. 
- Prisoner's dilemma is also addressed through material abundance.
